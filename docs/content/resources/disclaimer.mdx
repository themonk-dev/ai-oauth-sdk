## Using a token may not be permitted

A provider's terms of service govern what you may do with a credential issued to their own client,
and several of them restrict it. Obtaining a token with this library does not mean you are allowed
to use it, and nothing here grants you a permission the provider has not.

Read the terms of service, the acceptable use policy and the developer policy for every provider you
sign into, and decide for yourself whether your use is covered. If it is not, register your own
OAuth client where the provider offers one, or use an API key instead. See
[credentials](/docs/resources/credentials) for where self-registration is possible.

**Use this entirely at your own discretion and at your own risk.** The maintainers of this project
cannot and do not make any representation about whether a given use is permitted.

## No official support for these flows

None of these vendors publishes a supported, documented OAuth integration for third-party clients.
What this library encodes was observed from the vendors' own publicly distributed command-line
tools, Codex, Claude Code, Gemini CLI, the Copilot CLI, grok-cli and qwen-code, and from the
standards those tools implement: RFC 6749, RFC 7636, RFC 8252, RFC 8628, and OpenID Connect
Discovery.

That has two consequences worth planning around.

**Nothing here is stable by contract.** Endpoints, scopes, client ids and response shapes can change
or stop working at any time, without notice, and without it being a bug in this library. The
[custom provider](/docs/providers/custom) escape hatches exist partly so you can route around a change
without waiting for a release.

**No vendor owes you support for it.** Do not open a ticket with a provider about behaviour caused
by this library.

## Client identifiers

The values in `publicClientIds` are public OAuth client identifiers, extracted from software the
vendors distribute. OAuth is designed so that publishing a public client id is safe, and they are
not secrets.

They are still someone else's identifiers. Using one means presenting your application as that
vendor's CLI. That is why no provider defaults to one and you have to name it explicitly. Before
shipping it in anything, read the provider's terms of service and developer policy, and register
your own client wherever one is on offer. The [credentials](/docs/resources/credentials) page lists where
that is possible.

One client secret is bundled, Gemini's, because its token endpoint refuses an installed-app exchange
without one and those secrets are documented as non-confidential. It is overridable like
everything else.
